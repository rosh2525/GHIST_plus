# Every approach we tried, side by side, and why each one won or failed

All numbers are median per-gene PCC with measured RNA on cells not used for fitting, unless stated otherwise.
"Cores" = RNA measured in a few TMA-like disks of the same slide (the paper's core-to-WSI setting).

## Timeline and verdicts

## The pattern across all of them

1. **What worked changed the input or the output form, not the biology priors.** The biggest, most robust gain (soft output) needs no RNA and no training. Heavier linear corrections (v3, HAC, image features, priors) matched or lost to the authors' shrunk ridge once it got the better input. The one heavier model that won, BioHAN, won through its two-part output; its ablations show the biology components contribute nothing measurable.
2. **Sparse, zero-inflated targets punish anything that clips.** v1 calibration, the v2 MLP and the export's top-K gate all lose ranking through ties at zero. Methods that stay graded (soft output, HAC's strictly positive output) avoid this.
3. **More local RNA beats cleverness.** v2 showed ridge needs 20k+ well-spread cells; realistic cores (13–31k cells) are just enough for a regularised linear correction, not for an MLP or a 2nd image encoder.
4. **Testing against the right baseline mattered.** v3 looked like a success against raw GHIST+ and only failed when compared with the authors' own released correction. From P0 onward, every method was compared with that baseline.
