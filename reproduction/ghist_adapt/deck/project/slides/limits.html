The problem

Where GHIST+ falls short

Hard on/off export

Only the top-K cells per gene are ‘on’; the rest are set to 0 (86% of values). Graded states become absence.

Miscalibrated on new slides

Predicted spread 3× the measured one; a ‘30% chance expressed’ is not 30% (error 0.12).

No per-cell uncertainty

Users cannot tell a confident prediction from a guess.

Core anchoring is a bolt-on

A ridge tool corrects the gated output, tuned on randomly chosen neighbouring cells.

Low whole-panel accuracy

Median gene PCC 0.07–0.11 on external slides; headlines use the top 20 genes only.

Costly to adapt

Retraining takes 72–120 GPU-hours; genes in under 1% of cells stay unpredictable.

Measured on the authors’ released Breast5K and ATERA predictions (86%, 3×, 0.12 are Breast5K). Training cost from the GHIST+ manuscript.

The paper has no limitations section, so these are what we measured. The first one is the key biological point: GHIST+ internally models a probability that a gene is switched on, but the exported file forces a yes-or-no decision. Real tissue has graded states, and Xenium misses transcripts, so a measured zero is often not true absence.
