The authors’ own correction, applied to the soft output instead of the gated export, wins 27 of 27 runs (+12–15%, every 95% CI above 0). Our hurdle-aware HAC wins 20 of 27; it gives the best spatial maps on two of three combinations, but fails our pre-declared test on the pan-cancer model.

RRC-on-soft was a pre-listed ablation, promoted after the results. Its trade-off: co-expression structure (CMD) slightly worse than the authors’ default.

Four 1 mm² cores, about 13 to 31 thousand measured cells, evaluated on the rest of the slide with a 200 micrometre buffer. The pan-cancer row is a second released GHIST+ checkpoint, run on free Kaggle compute. The key message: what matters is which GHIST+ output you correct, not the correction model. HAC was our pre-declared primary method and passed on two of three combinations; we report its failure on the third.
