Our three fixes, and why they work

1 · Soft hurdle output

Use P(expressed) × amount for every cell instead of the top-K on/off export. No RNA needed.

Biology: cell states are graded and Xenium misses transcripts; a probability keeps gradients a yes/no cut erases.

2 · Core-guided correction

With four measured 1 mm² cores, correct the soft output rather than the gated export (authors’ ridge or our hurdle-aware HAC).

Biology: every tumour runs its own programmes (receptor status, immune infiltration); co-expressed genes in the cores reveal them.

3 · Calibrated uncertainty

A trustworthy probability that a gene is on, plus a 90% range for its amount where it is on.

Biology: it answers the question asked of a marker: how likely is it on in this cell, and how strongly?

Kept honest: protocol frozen before every run · Breast5K for development, ATERA untouched for confirmation · compared with the authors’ own correction · cores chosen without looking at RNA · 3 repeats, 95% bootstrap CIs

Each fix maps to a biological reason. Fix 1: GHIST+'s exporter makes a hard yes-or-no call, but expression in tissue is graded, and Xenium detection is probabilistic, so a probability is the biologically correct output. Fix 2: a model trained on other patients cannot know this tumour's programmes, such as hormone-receptor status or how infiltrated it is; a few measured cores reveal which genes go together here. Fix 3: biologists ask whether a marker is on and how strongly, so we give a calibrated probability and a range.
